# The scored splits contain whole blanked cross-sections, and the key samples ~12% of its rows from them

Date: 2026-10-25
Competition: 2026-ieee-big-data-traffic-flow-bench
Finding: A mask regime does not only thin out links: it also blanks *every* link over a contiguous band. Probed directly on published validation partitions of D12_I5_N (2031-03): an R1 day has 36 of 288 slots (12.5% of the day, slots 141-176 = 11:45-14:20) where all 258 links are blank, an R2 day has 18 (6.25%), an R3 day has none. Over the month's regime mix (8 R1 / 13 R2 / 10 R3) that is ~6% of the day's slots. The submission key is not "all blank cells": on the R1 day it holds 10,402 of the 16,164 blank-and-eligible cells, and 1,286 of those key rows (12.4%) sit inside the all-blank band. Population that to the whole panel: roughly 6% of the scored state cells have no observed neighbour of any kind - no temporal neighbour within any useful horizon and no spatial neighbour at their own slot - so the only available signal is the link's weekday/slot profile.
Two implementation consequences, both silent failures:
1. `pivot_table(index="timestamp", columns="link_id")` **drops** a timestamp whose values are all NaN. A per-day grid built that way has 252 rows on an R1 day instead of 288 and every downstream array-indexed feature either crashes on a shape mismatch or, worse, silently misaligns. Rebuild the grid against the full sorted timestamp index of the day and reindex every pivot to it.
2. Those cells must be answered at roughly profile quality. Measured on the 22,605 band cells of three real June days (band imposed synthetically so truth is known): the weekday/slot profile gives speed RMSE 17.35 km/h and flow 433.9 vph; the previous day's same slot 21.09/568; the next day's 29.50/1159; the mean of the last pre-band and first post-band observation 17.03/472. A model that zero-fills its neighbourhood features instead answers 17.8/496 - no better - and one that swaps the profile into the missing features answers 15.6/495, i.e. slightly better on speed and level on flow. Filling missing neighbours with the cell's own profile (and leaving the distance features at horizon+1 to say "nothing nearby") is therefore the right encoding, not a zero fill.
Evidence: probe of kaggle_public/corridors/D12_I5_N/validation/mainline_states_masked/mask_regime=R{1,2,3}/synthetic_mainline_2031_03_{01,02,04}.parquet: all-blank slots 36/18/0 of 288, key rows inside the R1 band 1,286 of 10,402; band_signal_test.py on 2030-06-21..23, band slots 141-176: profile 17.35/433.9, prev-day 21.09/568.3, next-day 29.50/1158.7, edges 17.03/472.0 (n=15.6-22.3k cells); band_regime_test.py D12_I5_N band 141-176: model 17.75/495.8 (zero fill) vs 15.56/494.7 (profile fill).
Applies when: imputing a masked panel whose mask regime can remove a whole time band. Check the per-timestamp observation count before trusting a pivot-built grid, and never let a spatial/temporal feature default to 0 for a cell that has no observation at all.
