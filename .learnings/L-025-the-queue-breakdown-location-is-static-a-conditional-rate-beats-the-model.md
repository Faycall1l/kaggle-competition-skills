# The queue breakdown location is STATIC, not dynamic - a per-link conditional rate beats the learned model on the onset windows (0.126 -> 0.44 IoU out-of-sample)
Date: 2026-10-22
Competition: 2026-ieee-big-data-traffic-flow-bench
Finding: Task 2 is scored as a space-time IoU over two window conditions. The champion's learned model handles the `queue_ongoing` condition well but collapses on `queue_onset`, and the collapse is structural, not a tuning accident. Diagnosis on a 63-window proxy built from the train split (official scorer): the onset windows contain 2.5 queued cells on average and the champion predicts 1.29, IoU 0.126, while the ongoing windows reach IoU 0.620. Two measured causes: (1) the champion's onset slice trains on 801,864 rows with 80 positives (pos_rate 0.0001) because it samples every origin whose panel is queue-free, while the scored onset windows are by construction the rare origins where a queue actually appears in the next 30 minutes - a 16x base-rate mismatch; (2) the onset breakdown is not predictable from the dynamics at the origin. Measured within-window AUC of every observable for the onset: mean_speed 0.689, min_speed 0.674, last_speed 0.643, margin 0.597, flow_slope 0.552, speed_slope 0.555, occupancy 0.313, density 0.362 - and every sign is inverted relative to the ongoing condition, where occupancy reaches 0.811 and speed falls to 0.257. What IS predictable is which link breaks down: over 24 June onset windows only 6 distinct links ever break down (L5N-059 16x, L5N-061 16x, L5N-104 14x, L5N-235 6x, L5N-237 4x, L5N-147 4x), and a per-link conditional rate P(link queued at T+30 | the panel was queue-free for 60 min) recovers exactly that set - for D12_I5_N the top three links score 0.71/0.68/0.64 and the next one 0.21. Predicted on the final step only (the organizer's selector puts the queue on T+30 by construction) this lifts the onset IoU from 0.126 to 0.79 in-sample and to 0.44 out-of-sample, taking the proxy S_queue from 0.373 to 0.539. The number of links to predict is read off the same gap rather than hard-coded - k = #{links with rate >= 0.5*max(rate)} - because the panels differ in size (D12_I5_N 258 links, D7_I10_E 146, D12_I5_S 100) and the rule reproduces each panel's best fixed k (3, 7, 3) on all three.
Evidence: 63-window June proxy, official score_task2.py: champion S_queue 0.3729 (onset 0.1264, ongoing 0.6195); + conditional prior 0.7056 (onset 0.7917); 66-window July proxy with the model trained on June only: champion 0.3655 (onset 0.1185) -> + prior 0.5241 (onset 0.4358); per-panel onset IoU at the self-tuned k: D12_I5_N 0.617, D12_I5_S 0.594, D7_I10_E 0.767; within-window AUC table above
Applies when: a per-window metric is dominated by a rare regime that the training distribution barely contains - measure the regime's own base rate before blaming the model, and look for a static (per-entity) predictor when the per-window observables are uninformative
